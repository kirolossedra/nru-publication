# D-006 — Separate source stop from simulation stop and choose pilot loads from calibration

- **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Status:** Implemented and verified for the Mission 3 pilot; the run-horizon limitation remains unresolved.
- **Context:** The example originally stopped sources, sinks, and the simulator at the same `simTime`. This made late packets indistinguishable from packets still in flight. Baseline 500 kbps was not a load range. The pilot requires load points informed by observed offered traffic, achieved throughput, occupancy, and queueing behavior.
- **Alternatives:** Keep simultaneous stop; infer a drain window without stopping sources; change packet size/topology; add a separate source-stop parameter and hold all radio/network variables fixed. For loads: select percentages of guessed capacity, or run a short rate ladder and inspect observed outcomes.
- **Selected:** Add `trafficStopTime` (negative/default means `simTime`) so sources stop before sinks/simulator; select 500 kbps, 2 Mbps, and 12 Mbps per enabled technology for the controlled pilot.
- **Reasoning:** The separate stop preserves default behavior and permits a measured drain. Calibration at 0.5/2/8/12 Mbps showed generated application rate close to configured rate, FlowMonitor IP throughput slightly above due IP bytes, occupancy increasing from 1–5% through 13–20%, and rising NR mean delay at higher successful rates. 12 Mbps was the highest tested rate that completed across three short calibration seeds; 14/16/20 Mbps cases asserted with negative scheduling delay. The three load points therefore span baseline/low, intermediate, and highest currently successful, not arbitrary utilization percentages. They do not establish channel saturation.
- **Consequences:** Pilot can account for packets not received after a 0.9-second post-source drain (the initially planned one-second interval was reduced to retain the same 4.9-second safe simulator horizon). Throughput uses source-active duration; occupancy is normalized by the full observation duration from app start through simulator stop. Calibration failures are preserved.
- **Known disadvantages:** A 0.9-second drain may still be insufficient at higher queueing; `not_received_by_stop` remains end-of-run nonreceipt, not definite drop cause. The highest selected load only reached about 20% occupancy in calibration and does not establish saturation. Three seeds at high load produce a small extreme-tail count even when pooled P99.9 passes the 10,000-sample reporting gate.
- **Evidence:** `patches/mission3-traffic-drain.patch`; `results/mission3/calibration/`; `configs/mission3-pilot.json`; Mission 3 report.
